## What we did

The converter takes two STL files at once: a figure and the base it was sculpted for. It guesses which file is the base, finds where the figure belongs on it, sets it down there and merges the two into one mini before the detail levels are made. The page shows where the figure was set ("Set in the 13 mm recess") and lets the user move, raise, lower and turn it, swap the two files or remove the base. Nothing converts again until the user presses Apply. The work started from Fable's design pass (the design note `docs/design/base-file.md`, draft PR #89) and was built on the same PR.

## Why

Most sculpted minis ship as two files, and until now a person had to join them in another program first (Phase 1 spec, story 10).

## How

- **Which file is the base.** A base was printed on its underside, so it has one: the up detection of #72 already measures that. With one flat underside that file is the base; with two, the lower one (height over width), with a warning; with none, the pair is refused with a message that says what to do.
- **The top as a height map.** The base's top is read into a grid of 0.5 mm cells, the highest surface seen from above. Holes through the base are cells nothing covers inside the outline.
- **A socket is a basin.** A recess, a hole and a slot all hold water when rained on. The classic trapping-rain-water flood from the outline inwards gives each cell the level water would stand at; wet cells 0.4 mm deep or more form basins.
- **Matching.** The figure's contact footprint is its vertices within a band of its lowest point: the soles, a puddle, or the end of a peg. The basin whose size fits that footprint best wins; without one, the flattest patch of the top the size of the footprint, nearest the centre. A long footprint over a long basin is turned to align (a tab into a slot).
- **Dropping.** The figure is lowered straight down until any vertex touches the lowest top among the 3 × 3 cells under it. The neighbourhood is the fit tolerance: without it a peg the size of its hole would sit on the rim.
- **Merging.** The figure's vertices come first in the merged mesh. The page can then split the full-detail mesh at the figure's last triangle and move the figure part in the viewer without any extra data.
- Everything uses + − × ÷ and square roots only, so the regression pair `figure-on-base` (a generated figure on a 12 mm puddle, in a 32 mm base with a 14 mm recess) gives the same figures on every machine. No existing baseline case moved.

## Problems and how we solved them

- **A square base lay on its side.** A lone slab has a flat face on more sides than its underside: the wall of a square base covers its side footprint as fully as the underside covers the top view, and the single-file detection took the wall. **Fix:** for the files of a pair, the thinnest of the flat axes wins, and on that axis the side with the larger coverage. Single files are unchanged.
- **The recess reads a cell too small.** The rim's vertices stamp the outermost ring of cells, so the 14 mm recess measures 13 mm. It does not change the choice. It shows in the placement line.
- **The design's test expectations did not fit their fixtures.** The note expected the generated figure without a base to go into the recess. Its lowest point is a 2 mm round body end, which by the fit rule fits no 14 mm recess and lands on the flattest patch (the recess floor, centred, but called "flat"). The regression case uses a figure on a puddle instead. The note also had peg length and hole depth the wrong way round in one sentence; the tests prove the physical case.
- **Full e2e runs timed out on this PC**, in different tests each run, with parallel workers baking at once. With one worker all 27 pass.

## Dead ends

None dropped yet. See Still open for what the library survey found.

## Numbers

- Place step on a 5.6 M-triangle corpus figure over a 1 M-triangle generated base, development PC, Node, `scripts/measure-place.mjs`: median 236 ms over 7 runs (181–541 ms), against a 300 ms budget. Precomputing the lowest top around each cell once, instead of reading nine cells per vertex, gives the same result with less work.
- Survey of 60 figure + base pairs from the PM's licensed library (counts only): 40 have both files standing the same way up. 13 of those 40 are exported registered: the figure dropped from where the file puts it lands within 0.3 mm of its height in the file. 25 are exported apart. 5 of the 60 are refused, and about 10 bases come out upside down.

## Still open

Stopped at the corpus pairs with three questions for the PM on PR #89:

- **Registered pairs.** For those, the heuristic does worse than the files' own placement: one figure was lifted 15.6 mm onto a rock instead of 3.2 mm. Proposal: keep the files' placement when it fits, and run the heuristic otherwise.
- **Rimmed and hollow bases.** A rim under a hollow base covers 11–13 % of the footprint, under the 15 % a base needs, so the pair is refused. A flat top over a hollow underside wins the detection and turns the base upside down.
- **No peg-and-hole or recess pairs in the library.** The three kinds of pair the issue asks for need other sources, or the generated fixtures.

The PM's visual judgement on the comparison sheets, and the wording, follow once the pairs are chosen.

## Story angle

"Rain on a base": finding a mini's socket by asking where water would pool. And an honest second act: the real files were already assembled more often than anyone expected.
