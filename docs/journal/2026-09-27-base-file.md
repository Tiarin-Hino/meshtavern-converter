## What we did

The converter takes two STL files at once: a figure and the base it was sculpted for. It works out which file is the base, stands both the right way up, finds where the figure belongs, sets it down and merges the two into one mini before the detail levels are made. The page says where the figure went ("Set in the 13 mm recess", "Set where the files put it", "Set over the middle of the base"). The user can move, raise, lower and turn the figure, swap the files or remove the base; nothing converts again until Apply. A feedback mode lets the PM step through pairs, correct each one and keep the result as a record, and every rule change is scored against those records. The work ran from Fable's design pass (`docs/design/base-file.md`, 2026-09-27) through two builds on Opus and an overnight research pass on Fable in between, all on PR #89.

## Why

Most sculpted minis ship as two files, and until now a person had to join them in another program first (Phase 1 spec, story 10).

## How

- **Which file is the base.** A base was printed on its underside, so it has one. The first build used the up detection of #72; real bases broke it (see Problems). Now a flat underside counts within 2 mm of the lowest point. Of several flat sides the thinnest axis wins, so a square slab does not lie on its wall. When both faces are flat, the hollowed one is the underside. A base exported tilted stands on its dominant plane. The file with an underside is the base; with two, the lower one; with none, the pair is refused with a message that says what to do.
- **Files exported together.** Some pairs come in one coordinate frame, the figure already standing on the base. The figure is turned the base's way and dropped from where the file has it. When it lands within 0.3 mm of the height the file gives it, that placement is kept, and the figure stands the way the base does.
- **A socket is a basin.** The base's top becomes a height map in 0.5 mm cells. The classic trapping-rain-water flood finds every region that would hold water at least 0.25 mm deep. A basin is a seat for this figure when its size fits the figure's lowest points (soles, a puddle, a peg's end) and it is compact; gaps between cobbles are thin and winding. Without a seat, the flattest patch the size of the soles, nearest the middle.
- **The centre guard.** When that choice would put the figure's box far from the middle of the base (more than 30 % of its width), or the figure's lowest points are wider than the base (wing tips), the box is centred on the base instead.
- **The figure's own axis.** A figure made for a separate base has its soles cut flat at one plane. When that cut is clear (at least 1 mm² and four times any other side, issue #90), it decides the figure's up; otherwise the detector's guess stands.
- **Dropping and merging.** The figure is lowered until a vertex touches the lowest top among the 3 × 3 cells under it (the fit tolerance: a peg the size of its hole goes in). Figure first, then base, in one mesh, so the page can split the full-detail mesh and preview a move without extra data.
- **Feedback and score.** `npm run feedback` opens each pair in Chrome. The PM uses the page's controls and presses R (right), S (save the placement), K (skip) or Escape. A record (axis, the figure's box centre and lowest point in the base's frame) and a three-view sheet are written per pair. `npm run score-placements` places every recorded pair with the current code in Node (read to place only, 21 s for four pairs) and reports the axis and the distance in millimetres. The corpus report carries the same score and a placement sheet per pair.

## Problems and how we solved them

- **Base undersides are hollow.** A rim touches the table, the inner face sits 1–1.5 mm up, or a lip pokes 0.2 mm below. The 2 % band (0.1 mm on a 5 mm base) saw only the rim: 5 of 60 library pairs were refused, about 10 bases came out upside down, and two corpus pairs were refused. **Fix:** the 2 mm band (the research found 860 of 864 library bases with it), and the base outline measured in the same band (one base measured 13.5 mm instead of 50 mm).
- **A plain disc could flip.** The research proposed that the side with more basins is the underside when both faces are flat. On the generated recess base (a flat disc with a 14 mm seat on top) that turned the base upside down. **Fix:** only a hollow covering half the footprint or more counts as an underside.
- **The weak print cut.** The design also let a weaker cut (0.3–1 mm²) compete with the detector, with the pair's placement choosing. With the note's measure (the share of soles touching the base) it turned the bat of the corpus upside down, and the one figure it was meant for is a registered pair. It is switched off by one constant, with a question for the PM.
- **Foot recesses are shallow.** They are 0.3–0.5 mm deep; the first 0.4 mm threshold missed most. 0.25 mm finds them; 0.15 mm finds many false ones.
- **A tilted base measured 6.8 mm wide.** Faces inside the base facing the same way tilted the dominant plane by 0.03°. **Fix:** the plane's last refinement uses only faces within the 2 mm band.
- **Apply moved the figure up or down.** A move or turn was dropped again on Apply, while the preview kept the height, so a figure moved from a recess onto its rim rose after the user approved it (review of #89). **Fix:** the height is the detected spot's, moved or not; Raise and Lower set it.
- **The recess reads a cell small** (13 mm for 14 mm): the rim's vertices stamp the outermost ring. It does not change a choice.
- **Local e2e runs timed out** in different tests each time with parallel workers baking at once. With one worker all 28 pass.

## Dead ends

The research's surface match (slide the figure's underside over the top, score the soles touching) was never better than seat-or-flattest on the rendered corpus and worse on two pairs, at 1–60 s a pair. Trying the figure in all six axes against the base picked the right one once in 15. Finer height-map cells and per-sole patches changed nothing useful. The library's one-piece files join a figure's parts, not the figure with its base, so there is no automatic ground truth.

## Numbers

- Corpus, 15 pairs (development PC, Chrome, no bake): all convert. Spot kinds recounted from `out/corpus-70b/results.json` on 2026-09-29: 4 kept where the files put them (flying-04, large-03, quadruped-01, swarm-01), 5 in a seat (flying-02, humanoid-01, humanoid-02, humanoid-03, and large-02 in its 12.5 × 18 mm footprint recess), 2 on the flattest patch (mounted-02, swarm-03), 4 centred by the guard (flying-01, large-01, mounted-01, mounted-03). An earlier tally in this entry and on the PR summed to 17; it was read from notes, not counted.
- Place step on the largest corpus pair (5.6 M-triangle figure, 0.4 M-triangle base, development PC, Node, `scripts/measure-place.mjs`): median 299 ms over 7 runs (247–386 ms), budget 300 ms. The first build took 236 ms on a generated base; the registration test and the centre guard add passes over the figure.
- Research (Fable, 2026-09-28/29): 1,328 library pairs inventoried, 864 analysed; 59 % of bases have no basin 0.4 mm deep, 12 % a seat for their figure, 3 % a through hole. The build's survey of 60 pairs: 13 of 40 same-way-up pairs exported registered.

## Story angle

"Rain on a base": finding a mini's socket by asking where water would pool. The second act is what real files taught: hollow undersides, files exported already assembled, and a PM's two hand placements beating every scoring rule.
