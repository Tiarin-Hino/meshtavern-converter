## What we did

The page and the library's two views (the questions a conversion asks, the painting view) now take every colour, font and control from the table application's design system instead of the spike's dark palette. The page follows the computer's light or dark setting, a switch in the bar picks Light, Dark or System, and the 3D stage stays dark in both. The fonts and stylesheets come from the page itself, no other site. Restyling the page's own elements on the components is the next item, #144.

## Why

The PM decided to publish the page before the table exists (#140), looking like the same product as MeshTavern (spec `docs/specs/look-and-feel.md`, L1). The design pass (#142, `docs/design/look-and-feel.md`, merged) chose a byte-for-byte copy of the application's theme here, kept equal by a test in the application, and both themes with a dark stage.

## How

- **The theme's home.** The application's `tokens.css` (the roles, both themes through `light-dark()`), `components.css`, `theme.js` (the switch) and the two fonts with their licences moved from the mockups' folder to `public/theme/`, served at `/theme/`, byte-equal to the application's `main` at `791425e`. `src/theme-files.test.ts` pins the files and the application's budgets.
- **A colour check.** The application's `scripts/check-colours.mjs`, copied, scans `src/`, `public/theme/` and `index.html` in `npm run check` and fails on any colour literal outside `tokens.css`, but for the paint (the look presets' coats, the painter's default colour) and one scene file.
- **The scenes** read `--scene-backdrop` and `--scene-grid` once from the page root when they start (`src/lib/three/scene-colours.ts`), as the application's table does, with the tokens' values as the fallback. The grid is the role's white over the backdrop: the centre lines at its alpha, the others at half.
- **The question view's template** carries the components' classes (`btn`, `segmented`, `input`, `field`, `check`, `chip`), so it looks the same in the application; its stylesheet keeps the layout and a few parts the components lack. The up axis became a segmented group of six buttons.
- **Icons** are inline SVG from `lucide-static` 1.53.0 in two committed modules (`npm run icons`, regenerated by a test). Importing the package at run time, as the application does, would have made it a dependency of every consumer.
- **The page's stylesheet** reads the roles only; its own controls keep their old rules, on the roles, until #144.

## Problems and how we solved them

- **Issue numbers read as colours.** `#119` in `index.html`'s comments and "#127" in a dev tool's title matched the hex pattern. **Cause:** the finder had no HTML mode. **Fix:** HTML comments are blanked, and a hex of digits alone counts only after a quote, `:` or `=`, as the application already does for TypeScript.
- **A texture format named `'red'`** was flagged as the colour red. **Cause:** a dead option in the painter's render-target helper. **Fix:** removed.
- **The page's custom properties shadowed the theme.** `--line`, `--text`, `--accent`, `--accent-soft` and `--panel-width` are also roles of `tokens.css`, and the page's sheet loads later. **Fix:** the colours went; the width became `--page-panel-width`.
- **Two `shimmer` animations.** The page's progress bar had one with the name of the components' skeleton animation, which it would have replaced. **Fix:** renamed.
- **The reduced-motion rule never applied in Chrome.** It listed a `::-moz-` pseudo-element with a WebKit one; Chrome drops the whole list. **Fix:** two rules.
- **The page's control rules would have restyled the view on the page only.** **Fix:** they now skip anything with a component class.
- **Small regressions the theme brought:** the view's small buttons fell to 26 px on a phone (a touch rule keeps 44 px), the switch made the phone's bar too wide (hidden there until #144's menu), a press on the axis already chosen would have answered again (ignored), the drag overlay's words were unreadable over the dark stage in the light theme (now on the application's banner).
- **The grid could not keep its old greys.** The design pass expected "within a step or two"; the spike's greys were bluish and imply a stronger white, so the centre lines are a little weaker than before. The test pins the new values.

## Numbers

- Colour literals found by the check before: 51 (26 in the three stylesheets, 25 in TypeScript and HTML, 2 of those false); after: 0.
- Fonts: 115,560 bytes (budget 120,000). CSS after gzip: 15,781 bytes (budget 40,000): tokens 4,068, components 5,251, page 4,105, questions 1,866, painting 491.

## Still open

- #144 restyles the page on the components: the bar, the cards, the done panel, the More menu with the theme on a phone, where Cancel and Confirm share a footer, and Pull apart as a pressed button.
- The application's companion change: the equality test over this copy, and dropping its `.import-stop` bridge, with its next pin bump.
- `light-dark()` needs Chrome 123, Firefox 120 or Safari 17.5; an older browser loses the colours.

## Story angle

Adopting another app's design system without forking it: a copied theme, a check that bans colour literals, and the 3D stage that stays dark when the page goes light. "One palette, two repositories."
