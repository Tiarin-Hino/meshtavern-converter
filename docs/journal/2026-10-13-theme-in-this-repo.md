## What we did

The page and the library's two views (the questions a conversion asks, the painting view) now take every colour, font and control from the table application's design system instead of the spike's dark palette. The page follows the computer's light or dark setting, a switch in the bar picks Light, Dark or System, and the 3D stage stays dark in both. Fonts and stylesheets come from the page itself. Restyling the page's own elements is #144.

## Why

The page is published before the table (#140) and must look like the same product as MeshTavern (spec `docs/specs/look-and-feel.md`, L1). The design pass (#142) chose a copy of the application's theme here, kept equal by a test in the application, and both themes with a dark stage.

## How

- **The theme's home.** The application's `tokens.css` (the roles, both themes through `light-dark()`), `components.css`, `theme.js` (the switch) and two fonts with their licences moved to `public/theme/`, byte-equal to the application's `main` at `791425e`. A test pins the files and the budgets.
- **A colour check.** The application's `scripts/check-colours.mjs`, copied, fails `npm run check` on any colour literal in `src/`, `public/theme/` or `index.html` outside `tokens.css`, the tests, the regression net's shapes, the paint and one scene file.
- **The scenes** read `--scene-backdrop` and `--scene-grid` once when they start (`src/lib/three/scene-colours.ts`), as the application's table does, with the tokens' values as the fallback. The grid is the role's white over the backdrop, the other lines at half its alpha.
- **The question view's template** carries the components' classes (`btn`, `segmented`, `input`, `field`, `check`, `chip`), so it looks the same in the application; its stylesheet keeps the layout and a few parts the components lack. The up axis became a segmented group of six buttons.
- **Icons** are inline SVG from `lucide-static` 1.53.0 in two committed modules, regenerated by a test; importing the package at run time would have made it a dependency of every consumer.

## Problems and how we solved them

- **Issue numbers read as colours.** `#119` in `index.html`'s comments and "#127" in a dev tool's title matched the hex pattern. **Cause:** the finder had no HTML mode. **Fix:** comments are blanked; outside its CSS a hex counts only as a value, never a link's `#id`, and named colours only inside its CSS, which a review made as strict as a stylesheet's.
- **A texture format named `'red'`** was flagged as the colour red. **Cause:** a dead option in the painter's render-target helper. **Fix:** removed.
- **The page's names shadowed the theme's.** `--line`, `--text`, `--accent`, `--accent-soft` and `--panel-width` are also roles of `tokens.css`, and the progress bar's `shimmer` keyframes had the name of the components' skeleton animation; the page's sheet loads later and would have replaced them. **Fix:** the colours went, the rest was renamed.
- **The reduced-motion rule never applied in Chrome.** It listed a `::-moz-` pseudo-element with a WebKit one, and Chrome drops the whole list. **Fix:** two rules.
- **Regressions the theme brought:** the page's control rules restyled the view on the page only (they now skip component classes); the view's small buttons fell to 26 px on a phone (a touch rule keeps 44 px); the switch overflowed the phone's bar (hidden there until #144); the drag overlay's words were unreadable in the light theme (now on the application's banner).
- **The grid could not keep its old greys.** The design pass expected "within a step or two"; the spike's bluish greys imply a stronger white, so the centre lines are a little weaker. A unit test pins the new values; an e2e test reads the stage's pixels and fails on the old backdrop.

## Numbers

Measured in the Claude Code cloud container (Node zlib, level 9; Playwright 1.63 with Chromium in software):

- Colour literals found by the check before: 51 (26 in the three stylesheets, 25 in TypeScript and HTML, 2 of those false); after: 0.
- Fonts: 115,560 bytes (budget 120,000). CSS after gzip: 16,025 bytes (budget 40,000): tokens 4,068, components 5,251, page 4,156, questions 2,059, painting 491.
- The #119 consumer check, repeated: a throwaway Vite project linking the library and the two theme stylesheets answered all four questions of a figure with its base by clicks, in both themes, without an error.

## Still open

- #144 restyles the page on the components: the bar, the cards, the done panel, the More menu with the theme on a phone, where Cancel and Confirm share a footer, and Pull apart as a pressed button.
- The application's companion change: the equality test over this copy, and dropping its `.import-stop` bridge.
- `light-dark()` needs Chrome 123, Firefox 120 or Safari 17.5; an older browser loses the colours.

## Story angle

Adopting another app's design system without forking it: a copied theme, a check that bans colour literals, and the 3D stage that stays dark when the page goes light. "One palette, two repositories."
