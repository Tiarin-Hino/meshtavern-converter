## What we did

We wrote down what Phase 1 must deliver: the Phase 0 spike turned into the component the MeshTavern table will use to take in minis. A person drops an STL and, without reading anything or choosing options, gets an upright, correctly sized, good-looking, baked mini, or a clear explanation of why not. The PM accepted the spec the same evening, including the order of the stories, and cut the list of test devices to two weak ones plus the development PC.

## Why

Phase 0 answered "can this work at all?" with yes. Phase 1 had to answer "is it good enough for someone who knows nothing about meshes?", and the implementing sessions needed a document that stands alone as a hand-over. The spec is [docs/specs/phase-1-converter.md](../specs/phase-1-converter.md); the epic is #40.

## How

The spec starts from four decisions the PM took on 2026-09-20:

- **No public launch in Phase 1.** The source repository is already public; "launch" means a deployed page and telling people about it. That waits until the table exists (publishing, #47, left the phase).
- When it is published, it goes to **GitHub Pages**.
- **Downloads offer the table and far levels only.** The close level (the most detailed LOD, "level of detail") is shown on screen but not exported.
- **The page collects nothing:** no analytics, no email field.

Around those the agent proposed an order of nine stories: first the net that catches regressions (#46), then the structural change everything else builds on, baked and compressed minis by default (#42 with #38), then correctness on real-world files (#43), scale and base (#44), a library API for the table application (#50, a new story), the page (#41), look presets (#45), and the blockers carried over from Phase 0 (#33 own xatlas build, #35 weak-device measurements, #38). Behaviour the agent suggested is marked _(proposal)_ in the spec; after acceptance it stands unless the PM changes it.

Exit criteria were given numbers: on the reference laptop an ordinary mini converts, baked and compressed, in 15 s or less; the largest corpus mini in 3 minutes or less (proposal); 100 baked minis run at 30 fps or more. The hand-over notes carry lessons from Phase 0: avoid stacked PRs, compare a document's headings before and after a scripted edit, and read the exit code of `npm run check`, not a filtered log.

PR #51 recorded the device decision: the Steam Deck and a further mid-range phone are dropped. The Iris Xe laptop and the Pixel 9 cover the low end; the development PC covers the middle and the high end. The spec's status went from "proposed, for PM review" to accepted, and the README now says there is no hosted version yet and links the Phase 1 spec.

## Problems and how we solved them

- **Problem.** The Phase 0 spec said "exit criterion 3 is not met yet" in its stress-scene section, while its later device section said it was met. **Cause:** the sentence was written before the laptop measurement and never updated. **Fix:** #51 points it to the device section.
- **Problem.** The Phase 0 conclusion still called Phase 1 "the public converter". **Cause:** written before the no-launch decision. **Fix:** reworded in #51.

## Dead ends

The first version of the spec put the parallel-worker unwrap (#34) out of scope, "unless the weak-device measurement in #35 shows the unwrap is too slow". A day later the corpus runs showed the unwrap was the problem even on the fast PC, and #34 came back in (see Later).

## Numbers

The device decision rested on the laptop and phone runs of the same day, recorded in #35:

- **reference laptop:** the generated 100 MB STL (2 M triangles) converts in 3.4 s, no tab crash; 100 minis at 60 fps (display cap), 60 fps up to 400 minis at table level. Benchmark panel, `npm run lan`.
- **Phone:** the same file in 5.5 s; 100 minis at 60 fps (display cap).
- Still owed after #51: a large mini with a 2K texture on the laptop.

## Still open

- The corpus had to grow to 20 to 30 minis; that is the PM's part (#46).
- "Ordinary mini" had no definition yet; #56 proposed one.

## Story angle

How a hobby project decides what "done" means before writing code: four product decisions, nine stories, numbers in the exit criteria. Title idea: "Not launching on purpose".
